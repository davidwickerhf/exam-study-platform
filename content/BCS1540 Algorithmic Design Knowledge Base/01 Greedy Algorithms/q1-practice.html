1. The Royal Cinema

You are the manager of the Royal Cinema, a single-screen movie theatre, and you want to show as many films as possible on the upcoming Cinema Day. You have a catalogue of n candidate films, where film i has a start time si and an end time fi (with si < fi). The theatre can show only one film at a time, and each film must be screened in its entirety.

You want to choose a subset S of the catalogue such that:

  • No two films in S overlap in time (the end time of one is less than or equal to the start time of the next).
  • |S| is as large as possible.

You may assume that:

  • Films are given to you in arbitrary order (not pre-sorted).
  • A film with end time exactly equal to the next film's start time is considered non-overlapping (you can show them back to back).
Example. If n = 6 and the films are at times (1, 4), (3, 5), (0, 6), (5, 7), (4, 9), (8, 11), you can show 3 films: (1, 4), (5, 7), (8, 11). Showing 4 films is not possible.

2. Hydraulic Network Construction

You are the chief engineer for the Kingdom of Aquatia, planning a water distribution system to connect n freshwater wells throughout the kingdom. You have a list of m proposed water pipes; pipe k would connect wells uk and vk at a construction cost ck (in royal coins). You want to install a subset of pipes such that:

  • Water can flow between any two wells, possibly via intermediate wells.
  • The total construction cost is minimised.

You may assume that:

  • The proposed pipe graph is connected (every well is reachable via some sequence of proposed pipes).
  • Pipe costs ck are positive integers.
  • Pipes are bidirectional — water can flow in either direction.
Example. With n = 4 wells and m = 5 pipes (1, 2, c=6), (1, 3, c=3), (2, 4, c=4), (3, 4, c=5), (2, 3, c=2), the cheapest network installs pipes (1, 3), (2, 3), (2, 4) for total cost 9.

3. Airport Gate Allocation

You are the operations manager for a busy regional airport. Today the airport has n scheduled flights, where flight i has a known arrival time ai and departure time di (with ai < di). Each flight must occupy a single gate continuously throughout its ground period [ai, di] — including taxiing in, disembarking, refuelling, boarding, and taxiing out. Two flights cannot share a gate during overlapping ground periods, but a gate can be re-used by another flight as soon as the previous flight has departed.

You have an unlimited number of gates available, but each open gate incurs an operational cost. You want to determine the minimum number of gates required to accommodate all n flights today, and to output a valid assignment of each flight to one of the gates.

You may assume that:

  • Flights are given to you in arbitrary order (not pre-sorted).
  • A gate may be re-used at the exact moment a previous flight departs (a flight departing at time d does not conflict with a flight arriving at time d).
  • Every gate is identical.
Example. With n = 4 flights at ground periods (9, 12), (10, 11), (11, 13), (12, 14), you need 2 gates: gate A hosts (9, 12) then (12, 14); gate B hosts (10, 11) then (11, 13).

4. Lighthouse Coverage Along a Coastline

You are the Lighthouse Commissioner for the Kingdom of Maritima, planning to install warning beacons along a straight coastline of length L. There are n known shipwreck hazards located at positions 0 ≤ p1 ≤ p2 ≤ … ≤ pn ≤ L along the coast. Each lighthouse has a fixed warning radius r > 0 — a lighthouse installed at position x warns ships of any hazard at position p with |p − x| ≤ r.

You may place lighthouses at any real-valued position along the coast (not necessarily at hazard positions), and you want to determine the minimum number of lighthouses to install so that every hazard pi is within warning range of at least one lighthouse.

You may assume that:

  • The hazard positions p1, …, pn are given to you in sorted order.
  • The warning radius r is the same for every lighthouse.
  • A lighthouse's coverage on the coast is the interval [x − r, x + r], including both endpoints.
Example. If n = 4 with hazard positions 1, 4, 9, 12 and warning radius r = 2, you need 2 lighthouses: one at position 3 (covering hazards at 1 and 4) and one at position 11 (covering hazards at 9 and 12).
